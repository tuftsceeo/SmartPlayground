# Teacher prompts that rebuild the example games

Each prompt describes a game in plain words, without naming the example it imitates. Game play
comes first, then what the kids touch or do, then what the lights show. Rainbow (battery) and
Jump In (Splat) are left out.

## Jump

> The game should be called "Jump Demo". Make a jumping game. Every time a kid jumps with the wand, one more light turns on, filling the
> lights up from the bottom like a glass of water. Only count a real jump, not a wiggle, and give
> them about a second between jumps. Pressing the button starts the count over at zero.

## Shake Rainbow

> The game should be called "Shake Rainbow Demo". Make a shaking game where the wand glows one color. A gentle shake makes it red; shaking harder
> climbs through orange, yellow, green, blue and purple. The color never goes back down. It
> remembers your best shake, like a high score. Press the button to go back to the start and try
> again.

## Melody

> The game should be called "Melody Demo". Make a song-building game with one sticker for each note: C, D, E, F, G, A, B and high C. When
> a kid taps a note sticker, the wand plays that note and shows its letter, then adds one colored
> light for it, with a different color for each note, like a rainbow. The lights fill up in order so
> kids can see their song, up to 25 notes. Pressing the button plays the whole song back, with
> each light glowing brighter as its note plays. An "erase" sticker clears the song. While
> waiting, show a softly glowing music note.

## Cooking

> The game should be called "Cooking Demo". Make a pretend cooking game. Kids tap ingredient stickers (tomato, milk, cheese, flour, egg,
> butter and sugar) to put them in the bowl. Each ingredient flashes its own shape and color when
> tapped, then stays as one colored light so kids can see what's in the bowl. When they hold the
> button, show what they made: egg + butter makes a fried egg; flour, milk, egg and butter make
> pancakes; tomato, cheese and flour make pizza; add an egg to that for pasta; and flour, milk,
> egg, butter and sugar make a cupcake. If the mix isn't a recipe, show a question mark. Letting
> go of the button keeps the ingredients; tapping the "cooking" sticker again empties the bowl.
> While the bowl is empty, show a gently glowing flame.

## Freeze Dance

> The game should be called "Freeze Dance Demo". Make a freeze dance game for the whole group. One wand belongs to the leader, who taps a
> "caller" sticker; everyone else presses their button to join as a dancer. When the leader holds
> their button down, every dancer's wand shows a green "go" arrow and they dance. When the leader
> lets go, the wands turn to a red square and everyone must freeze. Anyone who moves is out and
> sees a blue sad face. If the leader shakes their wand, it's "keep moving" time with a purple
> dancing figure, and anyone who stands still is out. Kids who are out tap a "rejoin" sticker and
> press their button to come back in.

## Ice Cream

> The game should be called "Ice Cream Demo". Make a pretend ice cream parlor game. Holding the
> wand upright, kids press the button to pick a flavor: once for strawberry (red), twice for orange,
> three times for lemon (yellow), then mint (green), blueberry (blue), bubblegum (pink) and grape
> (purple); eight or more presses is vanilla (white). Each press adds a white light along the
> bottom so kids can count. To scoop, they flip the wand upside down and back up. The whole wand
> flashes the flavor's color with a happy two-note chime, then goes back to white, ready for the
> next customer.

## Multi Ice Cream (follow-up to Ice Cream)

Send this in the same chat, after Ice Cream Demo is made.

> Let's change the game so kids can build a cone with three scoops. Call it "Multi Ice Cream
> Demo". Picking a flavor stays the same, but while counting, show the white lights down the middle
> instead of along the bottom. Each flip adds a scoop that stays on the wand: the first fills the
> top two rows, the second the middle row, and the third the bottom two rows, each in its own
> flavor color. Keep the finished cone showing until the button is pressed again to start a new
> one.
